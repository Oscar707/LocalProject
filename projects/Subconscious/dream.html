02 The Expired Tea and the New Teeth

2026-11-01

recurring placeteethmirrorloss of voicenumber two

The dream

I go to get bubble tea at a shop. It is a fictional shop, and I visited it before in other dreams. The decoration is white, woody and pretty. I look for brown sugar bubble tea, but the one on the shelf is expired. Thus I order a new brown sugar bubble milk tea. Suddenly my teeth hurt. I say, "One second, I will go to the bathroom." Then I cannot say any words. I look in the mirror. Two teeth grow out from my upper palate. My bones split into two. My teeth are shaky.

The sequence

  1. The familiar shopComfort, a known inner place
  2. Look for the usual sweetDesire for the habitual comfort
  3. It is expiredThe old comfort is no longer good
  4. Order a new oneAdaptation: a fresh version of the same thing
  5. Teeth hurt, then no voicePain, then loss of expression
  6. Mirror: two new teeth, bones splitTransformation in the body, seen directly

The dream moves from comfort to transformation. The turn point is the expired tea.

Interpretation (Jungian)

The shop that comes backA place that returns in many dreams is a fixed location in your inner landscape. White and wood suggest something clean, natural and safe. Jung would see it as a sanctuary of the psyche: a place where the unconscious meets you. When a known place returns, pay attention: the unconscious uses it as a stage for a new message.
Brown sugar bubble teaA sweet, familiar pleasure. It is comfort, reward and care for yourself. In the language of the psyche, it is the "usual way to feel good."
Expired on the shelfThe ready-made comfort has passed its date. This is the key image. Something that nourished you before is now stale: a habit, a way to cope, a role, or a relationship pattern. You do the right thing: you order a new one. But the new order is still the same drink. The dream asks: is a fresh copy of the old comfort enough?
TeethTeeth bite, chew and break down food. Psychologically, they are the power to take in and process life (to "chew on" an experience) and to stand firm (to "have teeth"). Teeth also show in the smile, which is a part of the persona. Teeth dreams often come at times of transition, when a person feels less in control of how others see them.
Loss of voiceYou want to explain ("one second") but cannot speak. Here the ego loses the power of the word. This connects directly to Dream 01, where you could not write your name.
The bathroom and the mirrorThe bathroom is a private place, where we drop the public face. The mirror is the classic image of self-reflection. You go away from the others to see what happens to you. In Jung's terms, you leave the persona and meet an unknown image of yourself.
Two teeth from the upper palateNew teeth grow in the wrong place, from inside, near the place of speech. It is like teething: growth that hurts. The number two is important for Jung. Two is the number of the opposites. It shows the moment when one unconscious thing starts to divide, so that consciousness can see it. Something new grows, but it does not fit the old structure yet.
Bones split into twoBones are the structure that holds you up. A split structure can mean a conflict between two sides of yourself. In old initiation myths (which Jung studied), the body is taken apart and made new. It is frightening, but the image is of renewal, not of death.
Shaky teethThe foundation feels unstable. The old way to "bite" into life is loose while the new way grows.

The whole dream is an image of growing pains. It begins in a safe place with a habit that you know. The habit is expired. When you try to replace it with a fresh copy, the body reacts: pain, silence, and a strange new growth.

Jung would ask what the dream compensates. Possibly your conscious attitude is "I will continue with what comforts me, and simply renew it." The unconscious answers with a stronger image: real change is happening inside you, and it changes how you speak and how you take in the world. For a moment, this change makes you mute. The old voice does not work, and the new voice did not form yet.

Put next to Dream 01, the message becomes clearer. In both dreams you try to express yourself (a name, a sentence), and you cannot. In Dream 01, the tools failed. In Dream 02, the body itself changes. The psyche seems to go deeper: from the instrument to the person.

A Jungian question for the dream: not "Why do my teeth hurt?" but "What new thing grows in me that I cannot say out loud yet?"

The body level

Jung also said to check the literal level first. Do you grind your teeth at night, have real tooth pain, or eat much sugar? If yes, part of the dream can come from the body. Both readings can be true at the same time.

Only the dreamer can confirm a dream meaning. If a reading does not "click," it is not the correct reading. Your associations are more important than any list of symbols.

01 The Name I Could Not Write

2026-10-25

schooltestnamefailed tool

The dream

I am in school. We have a test. The first task is to write my name. I know how to write my name. But each pencil I take does not work. I try pencil after pencil, and I cannot write my name.

Interpretation (Jungian)

Jung said that a dream does not hide a message. It shows the present state of the psyche, as a picture. Thus we look at what the picture shows, not at what it hides.

The nameYour name is the label of the ego. It is the first word of the persona: the face you show to the world. At a test, the name tells the examiners "this is me."
School and the testSchool is the place of the collective: rules, grades, judgement from outside. A test asks, "Are you sufficient by their standard?"
The pencilsA pencil is a tool of expression. The ego uses it to put itself into the world. In the dream, the knowledge is there. Only the instrument fails.
Every pencilThe repetition is important. The unconscious does not let one other pencil solve the problem. This is not bad luck. It is a message that insists.

The central image is a gap: you know who you are, but the old tools cannot put it on paper. Jung would read this as a compensation. The conscious mind may think, "I know who I am. My identity is fixed." The unconscious answers, "The old way to state who you are does not work now."

This often occurs in a time of transition. The persona, the name that the collective gave you, becomes too small. Something larger, which Jung calls the Self, wants a new form of expression. The dream does not say that you lost your identity. It says that the old instruments of identity are now empty.

Also note the place. You are back in school, in an old stage of life, where others gave the grades. Possibly a part of you still waits for an outside authority to approve your name. The pencils that do not work can be a refusal: the psyche does not want to sign the old contract.

A Jungian question for the dream: not "Why can I not write my name?" but "Which new name, or which new instrument, wants to write itself?"

Jung taught that only the dreamer can confirm a dream meaning. If a reading does not "click," it is not the correct reading. Your associations are more important than any symbol list.
