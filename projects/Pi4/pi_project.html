CARD 02 Five ideas

AMorning Anchor

The Pi replaces the phone as your alarm. At wake time, a light fades on like sunrise. A small e-ink screen shows today's Bible verse, your one brick for today and your morning streak. You press one physical button when you are out of bed. The phone stays in another room.

Hits: sleep, dopamine, faith, "conquer the morning". You touch it every day.

BFamily frame (a gift)

A photo frame for a parent or grandparent. Family send photos and short messages to it from their phones. It shows them in rotation.

Hits: connection. Meaning comes from the other person. Lasts for years in their home.

CLife server

The Pi hosts all your HTML cards (workflow, sleep, trading...) with git history. You open them from your phone on home Wi-Fi. A 10-year archive of your iterations.

Hits: continuity, learning. Low emotional pull.

DFlight feeder (ADS-B)

A USB radio receives signals from planes. The Pi sends them to global flight-tracking networks. You help a public map.

Hits: contribution. But it runs alone, you do not touch it.

E24/7 trading paper bot

Your ATR signal runs on the Pi all day, paper trades, and logs each decision.

Hits: trading skill. Mostly money EV, which you asked to avoid.

CARD 04 The pick: Morning Anchor

It scores high because it attacks the root, not a symptom. Your sleep card and your dopamine card both end at the same place: the morning. The phone is the problem at night and in the morning. The Anchor takes the phone's two jobs (alarm, first screen of the day) and gives them to a device that only does good things.

Layers (from frequent change to seldom change)

Business rulesWake time, which verse plan, what "done" means, streak rules.
Domain rulesA morning = wake → button press → minutes to get up. A verse plan = day number → passage.
Tech stackPython on the Pi, GPIO for the button, an e-ink screen, a light (LED strip or a smart bulb), a log file.

Why it lasts

  • You use it 365 days a year, not only when you feel like it.
  • It makes data about you: each morning goes in a log. In a year you see your real pattern.
  • It is a system, not a goal. You do not need willpower at 6 AM. The light and the button do the work.
  • Later, idea B joins it: family can send a photo or a message that shows on the Anchor in the morning.

Extra hardware (check prices before you buy)

  • E-ink display for the Pi (for example, a Pimoroni Inky or a Waveshare HAT). E-ink has no glow, so it is good in a bedroom.
  • One big arcade-style push button and 2 wires.
  • Light: a WS2812 LED strip on GPIO, or a smart bulb you already have.
  • Optional: a small speaker for a soft sound.

CARD 05 Bricks (one brick = one weekend or less)

  1. Pi alarm with a button. Pi plays a sound at wake time. Press the button to stop it. Log the time. The phone leaves the bedroom on this day.
  2. Data log. Each morning writes one line: date, alarm, button_press, minutes_to_up. Pure calculation for the streak.
  3. Sunrise light. Light fades on over 20–30 min before the alarm. Morning light helps the body clock (links to sleep.html).
  4. E-ink screen. Shows the date, today's verse and the streak.
  5. Verse plan. A reading plan as data (day → passage). The screen shows the passage. You read it from a paper Bible, not a phone.
  6. "One brick today." At night, you write one line (from your brain dump) on the Pi from any device. In the morning it is on the screen.
  7. Morning report HTML. The Pi makes a weekly HTML card from the log: wake times, streak, minutes to get up. Same style as your other files.

Start with brick 1 only. It already removes the phone from the bedroom, which is the biggest win.

Note on blue light

Blue light is bad at night. In the morning, bright light helps the body clock. So: no light at night (e-ink), warm light for the fade, then open the curtains or walk outside (your morning plan).
